Chapter I

Earth

1 g · 14.7 psi · 21% oxygen

Where the fire learned everything it knows.

Voice-over

Fire knows which way is up.

On Earth, gravity writes a flame's shape. Hot gas is lighter than the air around it, so it rises. That plume pulls fresh air in from below, stretches the flame into a teardrop, and lifts glowing soot that turns it yellow.

21%oxygen in the air we breathe
14.7 psiair pressure at sea level
1 gthe gravity nearly all fire-safety rules were written under

Flashback

27 January 1967 · Apollo 1

During a ground test, a cabin filled with pure oxygen let a fire spread in seconds. Three astronauts died. Every spacecraft fire rule written since begins on that day.

Most of what we know about fire was learned at 1 g. Our story leaves it behind, one world at a time.

Chapter II

Mars

0.38 g · a third of the pull

The plume still rises. It just takes its time.

Voice-over

The flame starts to forget.

Look behind the text. The flame is shorter now, wider, bluer at the base. Air arrives more lazily and less soot glows.

The missing footage

NASA's PSI database holds no flame data at Mars or Moon gravity yet. Future studies, LUCI and FM2, aim to film that gap. E-FIELD Flames used electric fields to mimic partial gravity. Until then, engineers estimate limits between what Earth and orbit tests show.

Rocket engine fires are not in this film. Very fast combustion barely depends on gravity. This story is about fire in the rooms where crews live.

Chapter III

Moon

0.17 g · 10.2 psi · 30% oxygen

Richer air than home. A crew of two. A panel behind the wall getting warm.

Voice-over

The air here burns hotter.

With Artemis, people are going back to the Moon. Habitats and pressurized rovers are expected to breathe 30% oxygen or more at 10.2 psi or less. That's over 40% more oxygen than Earth air. The flame behind this text is running on it.

Nothing rises.

Oxygen reaches the flame only by drifting in from every side, or on a fan's breeze. The flame curls into a dim blue sphere. Look around: nothing on this screen is falling anymore either.
